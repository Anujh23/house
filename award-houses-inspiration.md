## Principles that repeat across these houses

1. Each side gets windows for its own sun: slits on the hot sides, big glass on the gentle sides.
2. Only 2–3 materials.
3. A court or light-well at the heart of the house.
4. The section does the climate work: double height, stack vent.
5. Deep openings and overhangs. The shade *is* the design.
6. A layered entry: screen, then garden, then door.
7. **One wow element; everything else quiet.**
8. Heavy mass on the west and SW, light and glass on the north and east.

## Avoid
- **Big glass on the west.** It turns the room into a greenhouse and needs about 1.5–2 tons of extra AC.
- **True bent/curved glass.** It costs ₹2.5–4k/sqft and replacing a broken pane takes weeks.
- **Real timber outside.** It cracks, gets termites and fades in the UV.
- **Frameless glass everywhere.** Dust storms mean weekly cleaning. Use it on one balcony only.
- **Thin cantilevers longer than 1.5 m.** They need deep beams and extra steel.
- **Fair-faced (exposed) concrete.** Local formwork isn't good enough to get it right.
- **Smooth pure-white paint.** It stains. Use textured or elastomeric paint and budget a repaint every 3–4 years.
- **Big lawns or pools.** Pilani gets about 450 mm of rain a year. Use gravel, neem and bougainvillea instead.
- **Fully open courtyards.** They let in dust. Add a pergola + mesh.
